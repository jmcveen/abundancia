## 1. Methodology

### 1.1 Analytical Framework

The sensitivity analysis employs four complementary approaches to evaluate risk and return variability:

| Method | Purpose | Output |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Scenario Modeling** | Discrete cases (Conservative, Base, Optimistic, Phase-1-Only Downside) with internally consistent assumptions | Full 10-year pro formas |
| **Single-Variable Sensitivity** | Isolate impact of each key variable while holding others constant | Relative IRR/EMx swing tables |
| **Monte Carlo Simulation** | Randomized iterations across all variables simultaneously | Probability distributions |
| **Stress Testing** | Extreme adverse scenarios to identify breaking points | Minimum viable returns |

*Note (July 2026): the base case below has been rebased to the current financial model. Where scenario deltas have not yet been exactly recomputed on the new base, results are expressed as approximate percentage swings relative to the base case, carried from the prior model run, and marked [TO CONFIRM] pending the full re-run.*

## 5. Tornado Analysis

The tornado analysis ranks each variable by its total impact range on project IRR when varied across its full sensitivity range while all other variables remain at base case.

### 5.1 IRR Impact Range by Variable (relative swings; [TO CONFIRM] on re-run)

| Rank | Variable | Approx. Relative IRR Swing Range vs. Base |
|------|----------|-------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Home Sale Pricing | ~ -68% to +23% |
| 2 | Absorption Rate | ~ -60% to +20% |
| 3 | Construction Costs | ~ -31% to +16% |
| 4 | Interest Rates | ~ -35% to +15% |
| 5 | Lot Pricing | ~ -10% to +7% |
| 6 | Permitting Timeline | ~ -14% to +4% |
| 7 | Inflation Rate | ~ -8% to +6% |
| 8 | Rental Occupancy | ~ -5% to +3% |
| 9 | Property Tax Rate | ~ -3% to +2% |
| 10 | Insurance Costs | ~ -2% to +1% |

### 5.2 Tornado Interpretation

**Home sale pricing and absorption rate dominate the risk profile.** Together, these two demand-side variables account for the majority of total IRR variability. This is typical of development-stage real estate investments where revenue timing and magnitude are the primary drivers.

**Construction costs and interest rates are the next tier of significance.** These are partially within management's control (through locked contracts, hedging, and phased procurement) and partially market-driven. The 10% project contingency ($28.6M) directly buffers construction cost variance.

**Rental occupancy has limited impact on project-level returns** because the rental portfolio represents ~11% of total revenue. However, rental operations provide cash flow stability during the development period and anchor the ~$7–8M/yr stabilized recurring revenue from 2032.

**Property tax and insurance are low-sensitivity variables** due to their relatively small share of total costs and limited variability range. These are included for completeness.

## 7. Stress Testing

*Stress results are expressed as approximate relative swings on the current base (33.5% IRR / 3.58x EMx), carried from the prior model run — [TO CONFIRM] pending exact recomputation.*

### 7.1 Individual Stress Scenarios

#### Stress Test A: 30% Price Reduction

| Metric | Base Case | Stressed (approx., [TO CONFIRM]) |
|--------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Per-type pricing | SFR ~$566/SF; condos ~$400/SF; tiny ~$416/SF | -30% across types |
| 10-Year Revenue | $400,565,031 | ~ -30% on residential/lot streams |
| LP IRR | 33.5% | ~ -68% relative swing (remains positive) |
| LP Equity Multiple | 3.58x | Materially reduced; capital preserved in prior run |

**Assessment:** A sustained 30% price decline exceeds the 2008–2010 Austin market correction (peak-to-trough ~18%). In the prior run, investors still recovered more than their capital under this stress; exact rebased figures are [TO CONFIRM]. Lot sales (highest margin of safety) and rental income stability support solvency.

#### Stress Test B: 50% Slower Absorption

| Metric | Base Case | Stressed (approx., [TO CONFIRM]) |
|--------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Delivery timeline | 2028–2031 | Sellout extends well beyond 2031 |
| LP IRR | 33.5% | ~ -60% relative swing (remains positive) |
| Additional capital | None | Possible bridge financing in mid years [TO CONFIRM] |

**Assessment:** Halving absorption extends the project timeline significantly but did not threaten capital preservation in the prior run. The phased construction approach limits exposure — management can defer later phases until earlier inventory is substantially absorbed, reducing carrying costs.

#### Stress Test C: 25% Construction Cost Overrun

| Metric | Base Case | Stressed (approx., [TO CONFIRM]) |
|--------|-----------|----------------------------------|
| Hard costs | $246,131,681 | ~ +$61.5M |
| LP IRR | 33.5% | ~ -31% relative swing (remains attractive) |
| Contingency absorption | — | 10% contingency ($28.6M) absorbs roughly half of the overrun before additional funding |

**Assessment:** A 25% cost overrun compresses margins significantly but the project retained attractive returns in the prior run because pricing is maintained and lot sales provide a cost-insensitive margin buffer.

#### Stress Test D: Combined Worst Case

| Applied Stress | Assumption |
|----------------|-----------|
| Pricing | -15% |
| Absorption | -30% |
| Construction costs | +15% |
| Interest rates | +200bps |
| Rental occupancy | Reduced |

**Assessment:** In the prior run, the combined worst case — a scenario comparable to a 2008-style crisis hitting during peak construction, with estimated probability <1% — still returned LP capital with a modest positive return, and the project did not require liquidation. Additional capital (mezzanine debt or follow-on equity) would likely be required. Exact rebased figures are **[TO CONFIRM]**. This is consistent with the model's Phase-1-only downside scenario, which independently shows positive LP returns if the project stops after Phase 1.

## 10. Mitigation Strategies

### 10.1 Risk Mitigation by Variable

| Risk | Mitigation Strategy | Residual Risk |
|------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Absorption slowdown** | Phased construction (build against demonstrated demand); 10,000-person interest list + 100+ investors/buyers/collaborators with zero paid marketing; conversion flexibility between for-sale and rental product | Moderate: market risk cannot be fully eliminated |
| **Price decline** | Per-type pricing with entry anchor from ~$450/SF; product mix flexibility (shift toward higher-margin lots); value engineering | Moderate: pricing is ultimately market-driven |
| **Construction cost overrun** | Fixed-price GC contracts where possible; 10% contingency ($28.6M); phased procurement; hempcrete supply-chain development | Low-to-Moderate: contracts transfer most risk |
| **Interest rate increase** | Rate management on the ~9% construction facility; pre-sale requirements before phase starts; adjustable phase timing | Moderate: macro risk with limited hedge options |
| **Occupancy shortfall** | Multi-platform distribution; corporate retreat partnerships (retreat market $31.8B → $73.7B by 2034); flexible stay formats (glamping/domes/camping) | Low: experience-hospitality demand growing |
| **Permitting delay** | No county zoning (mixed use by right); 12–16-month permitting estimate; experienced civil lead (Fred Haas, PE); MUD engineering underway; early TCEQ/OSSF and county floodplain coordination; conservation subdivision pathway for Houston toad / Lost Pines HCP | Low-to-Moderate |
| **Water availability** | Layered supply: Aqua WSC service; 7 existing ponds (+ more to be created); 6 wells + 13 storage tanks + filtration; rainwater capture on every building (~9–11M gal/yr at build-out); 11 atmospheric water generators; low-flow + greywater demand reduction; full watershed analysis by Symbiosis TX commissioned immediately upon entering due diligence | Low-to-Moderate: hydrology verification is first predevelopment spend |
| **Inflation spike** | Real asset hedge; escalation embedded in model; indexed contracts | Low: inflation benefits asset values |
