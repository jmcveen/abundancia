## 1. Design Philosophy

### 1.1 Conservation-Forward Development

Abundancia is designed around a foundational principle: **the land comes first**. Unlike conventional developments that maximize buildable area and relegate green space to residual parcels, Abundancia permanently preserves **184.5 acres (49%)** of the 376-acre property, with approximately **235 acres (62.5%)** dedicated to preserved land, regenerative agriculture, and permaculture gardens combined. Building footprints occupy only ~16.1 acres of structures (4.28% of the site), with ~746,000 SF of vertical construction and ~878,000 SF total developed area including roads.

This approach is not merely aesthetic. It is structurally integrated into every layer of the project:

- **Ecological**: Compliance with the Lost Pines Habitat Conservation Plan (LPHCP) for the federally endangered Houston toad via a conservation subdivision pathway
- **Financial**: Conservation land generates tax benefits while permanently protecting the premium natural setting that drives property values
- **Regulatory**: Bastrop County has no county-level zoning (mixed use by right), and low-impact, conservation-aligned design supports an efficient 12-16 month estimated permitting timeline
- **Market**: Wellness-oriented communities command 10-25% pricing premiums over conventional suburban development (Global Wellness Institute)

### 1.2 Design Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Land-First Conservation** | 184.5 acres permanently preserved; ~62.5% of the site in preservation, regenerative agriculture, and permaculture |
| **Biophilic Design** | Every structure oriented toward nature; preserved land woven through all neighborhoods |
| **Regenerative Infrastructure** | Solar + battery, rainwater harvesting on every building, greywater recycling, composting |
| **Walkable Core** | Mixed-use village center within walking distance of all residential zones |
| **Food Sovereignty** | 42 acres of regenerative agriculture plus 8.5 acres of permaculture gardens & barns |
| **Cultural Infrastructure** | Spaces for ceremony, creativity, education, and healing integrated throughout — including a 12-acre Event & Healing Center anchored by the renovated existing barn |
| **Fire-Resilient Design** | Defensible space zones, fire-resistant hempcrete construction, ember-resistant venting |
| **Non-Toxic Building** | Hempcrete (hemp-lime) primary construction — non-toxic, vapor-open, mold-resistant, carbon-sequestering |

### 1.3 Historical Context

The 2011 Bastrop Complex Fire burned 34,000 acres and destroyed 1,673 homes in this region — the worst wildfire in Texas history. Abundancia's master plan directly addresses this history through fire-resilient design (hempcrete wall assemblies carry 2+ hour fire resistance), native vegetation management, and defensible space standards that exceed Bastrop County requirements.

### 9.2 Water System

Abundancia's water strategy is a layered supply combined with demand reduction:

| Component | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Utility service | Aqua Water Supply Corporation (regional water utility) |
| Ponds | 7 existing water-retention ponds, with more to be created |
| Wells & storage | 6 wells + 13 storage tanks + filtration |
| Rainwater harvesting | Capture on every building — ~524,000 SF of roof area × 35.5"/yr × 0.623 gal/SF-in at 80-90% efficiency ≈ **9-11 million gallons/year at build-out** |
| Atmospheric water generation | 11 atmospheric water generators (drinking water) |
| Demand reduction | Low-flow fixtures + greywater recycling — demand below typical development |
| Verification | Full watershed analysis by Symbiosis TX commissioned immediately upon entering due diligence; hydrology and water feasibility are the first predevelopment spend |

## 14. Landscape Architecture

### 14.1 Design Approach

The landscape architecture for Abundancia embraces the existing Lost Pines ecosystem as the primary design element. Rather than imposing a landscape onto the site, the approach preserves, enhances, and extends the native ecology while creating functional outdoor spaces for community life.

### 14.2 Landscape Zones

| Zone | Treatment | Plant Palette | Irrigation |
|------|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Conservation corridors | Preservation + selective enhancement | Native loblolly pine, post oak, understory | None (natural) |
| Riparian buffers (dry creek beds) | Restoration + stabilization | Native riparian species, erosion control | None (natural) |
| Food forests | Designed permaculture systems | Fruit/nut trees, berry shrubs, herbs, groundcover | Drip (reclaimed water) |
| Community gardens | Raised beds + in-ground plots | Seasonal vegetables, herbs, cut flowers | Drip (rainwater + reclaimed) |
| Residential landscape | Native + adapted species | Native grasses, wildflowers, shade trees | Rainwater cistern |
| Village center | Hardscape + native accent plantings | Heritage oaks, native perennials, food plants | Drip (reclaimed water) |
| Sacred spaces | Minimal intervention + ritual plantings | Medicinal herbs, native ceremonial plants | None (drought-adapted) |
| Trail corridors | Stabilized surface + edge plantings | Native groundcover, pollinator plants | None (natural) |

### 14.3 Fire-Resilient Landscape Design

Post-2011 fire, all landscape design incorporates defensible space principles:

| Zone | Distance from Structure | Treatment |
|------|------------------------|-----------|
| Zone 1 (Immediate) | 0-5 ft | Non-combustible groundcover, no plants against walls |
| Zone 2 (Intermediate) | 5-30 ft | Low-growing, well-spaced, irrigated native plants |
| Zone 3 (Extended) | 30-100 ft | Thinned native woodland, removed ladder fuels |
| Zone 4 (Transition) | 100-200 ft | Managed native vegetation, fire breaks at roads/trails |

### 14.4 Water-Efficient Landscape Standards

| Standard | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| Turf grass | Prohibited (0% conventional turf) |
| Native species | Minimum 80% of all plantings |
| Irrigation source | Rainwater + reclaimed water only (no potable) |
| Mulch | 4" minimum organic mulch in all planted beds |
| Hardscape permeability | Minimum 50% permeable surface in all parking/plaza areas |
| Tree preservation | Heritage trees (>24" DBH) protected with 1 ft radius per inch DBH |
